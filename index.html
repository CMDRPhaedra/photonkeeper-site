Photonkeeper

Gets imaging sessions off a DWARF 3 or DWARF Mini and into a proper archive on your own drive — and puts them back byte for byte identical, so the telescope can still megastack and restack them.

macOS 14 or later · Windows 10 (1809) or later
Works with the DWARF 3 and the DWARF Mini. Both have been run against real data — the DWARF 3 by me, the Mini by an owner who put the whole round trip through it. If yours doesn't behave, or anything about it confuses you, tell me at support@chryse.co.uk — screenshots welcome.

What it does

  • Imports everything new off the telescope, SHA-256 checksumming each file as it copies rather than in a second pass.
  • Sends sessions back to the scope — filtered by target, date, exposure or gain — hashing on the way out and reading back to confirm.
  • Frees onboard storage, but only for sessions it has just verified against your archive.
  • Files M 42, M42 and NGC 1976 as one target instead of three folders.
  • Re-checks the whole archive for bit-rot later, remembers what it found, and can fetch damaged files back off the telescope.
  • Imports a whole card, or just the sessions you pick out of the list.
  • Removes sessions from the archive when you ask — to the Trash or the Recycle Bin, never straight out.
  • Every operation has a dry run that shows exactly what would happen first.

Session folders are never renamed and nothing inside one is ever altered — all the organising happens in the directories above them.

Try it without a telescope

The Mac app has a demo mode with sample data and a separate sample archive, so you can watch the whole round trip work before pointing it anywhere near your own sessions. Telescope → Try Demo Mode. The Windows app doesn't have it yet.

Requirements

  • macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later, Apple silicon or Intel — or Windows 10 version 1809 or later, x64 or ARM64.
  • A DWARF 3 or DWARF Mini, connected over USB.
  • Somewhere to keep the archive — an external drive is fine, and it's remembered even if you move or rename the folder.

The two are the same app and the same archive format: a drive written by one is read by the other, checksums and all. The Windows app is in English only, and has no demo mode.

Privacy

Photonkeeper collects nothing about you. There is no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no account to make, no licence to check and no tracking of any kind, and no third-party code in the app at all. Nobody, me included, learns what you photograph or when.

Your archive, its manifests and checksums, and a handful of small settings all stay on your own machine. The Mac App Store and Microsoft Store versions make exactly one kind of network connection — to your own telescope, on your own network. The Mac App Store build has the version check compiled out of it; the Windows build never had one written, because the Store handles updates. Copies downloaded from this page before Photonkeeper moved to the App Store add that one check, once a day, and it can be turned off.

Read the full privacy policy, which spells out every connection the app makes, exactly where your data lives, and what happens if you email me.
